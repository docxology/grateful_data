Introduction

What we mean by “the music never stopped”

The Grateful Dead performed roughly two and a half thousand concerts between 1965 and 1995. The public record of those concerts — setlists, recordings, ticket stubs, member rosters, composer credits, release histories, and decades of fan commentary — is one of the most extensively documented bodies of live performance in popular-music history. It is also unusually hybrid. UCSC’s Grateful Dead Archive gives the band a formal institutional home [ucsc_grateful_dead_archive], while the Internet Archive collection and Deadhead taping culture preserve the community-built sound archive and its metadata [internet_archive_gd_collection_policy; dead_net_taping_dead; wallace_cocreation_sound_archive]. The record is fragmented across primary databases, archival corpora, reference works, and a long tail of community spreadsheets and forum threads [reddit_data_sets; reddit_setlist_db; reddit_spreadsheet_archive]. This paper introduces The Music Never Stopped: a reproducible, citation-bound compendium that integrates the primary sources into a single schema, exposes them to standard data-analysis machinery, and exhibits a small but genuine category-theoretic structure latent in the performance graph.

Sources surveyed

We organize the cited landscape into four layers.

Primary setlist sources. Setlist.fm publishes a crowd-curated, API-backed register of every Grateful Dead show [setlistfm_gd]. The SetList Program at setlists.net covers 1965–1995 with an interactive interface [setlists_net_program]. The Mark Leone CMU archive [cmu_leone_setlists], derived from the Jerry Stratton database, is the ancestor of many community scrapes; Noah Gorstein has converted the abandoned HTML into an open SQLite database [noah_truckin_through_time]. GDsets combines setlists with ticket-stub and stage-pass images [gdsets_com; gdsets_dead]. The gdshowsdb repository ships a relational schema and a Ruby gem [gdshowsdb]; the GitHub topic gratefuldead is the meta-index that links these and other resources [github_topic_gratefuldead].

Primary lyric and song-attribution sources. Mark Leone’s lyric index at CMU [cmu_leone_lyrics] points to the Stratton database via FTP. Alex Allan’s whitegum.com is the canonical lyric and song finder for every song the Dead played, with composer/lyricist attribution and per-song performance data [whitegum_alex_allan]. Dodd and Trist’s The Complete Annotated Grateful Dead Lyrics supplies the authoritative interpretive and annotation context for lyric studies [dodd_annotated_lyrics]. We cite it as an external authority and store only pointer metadata; no lyric text is bundled. Hunter’s own retrospective account and community discussion are used only as context for songwriter roles, never as an override of the structured composer/lyricist fields [rolling_stone_robert_hunter_interview; reddit_lyrics_attribution].

Personnel, discography, recording, and reception sources. The official band site lists members and roles [dead_net_official]. Wikipedia’s Grateful Dead entry [wikipedia_gd] and discography [wikipedia_discography] anchor the biographical timeline; Britannica frames the wider cultural context [britannica_gd]. Ultimate Classic Rock’s lineup-changes guide [ucr_lineup_changes] provides a chronological narrative of personnel transitions. The Internet Archive’s Live Music Archive supplies recording metadata for the bulk of the live corpus [internet_archive_lma], and its Grateful Dead collection policy explains the special access and metadata posture of those recordings [internet_archive_gd_collection_policy]. The fifteen-songs-dataset curates 2617 soundboard recordings of 15 songs for music-information-retrieval research [fifteen_songs_dataset], while Wang et al.’s setlist-segmentation paper gives a broader MIR frame for why live setlist identification is non-trivial [wang_setlist_segmentation]. jerryPycia provides a Python query library over Dead show data [jerry_pycia]; the grateful-dead-reviews corpus collects long-form fan reviews [gd_reviews_corpus]. The gratefulstats.com front-end aggregates show/tune/venue statistics [grateful_stats]. The gratefuldata tutorial demonstrates an ETL pipeline using the Internet Archive together with ASCAP ACE composer data [grateful_data_tutorial]. The compendium draws composer/lyricist attributions from these sources and encodes them in data/archival/songs.json.

Historical, technical, and interpretive context. The supplied enriched bibliography is useful as a discovery map, but the map is not itself evidence. Each durable topic is therefore rebound to checked source families: the official dead.net biography and FoundSF’s Bob Weir oral-history page for formation and Acid Test context [dead_net_biography; foundsf_weir_psychedelic_sf], Smithsonian and UCSC for institutional archive status [smithsonian_institutionally_dead; ucsc_grateful_dead_archive], McIntosh for the Wall of Sound hardware [mcintosh_wall_of_sound], Marshall, JSTOR Daily, Meriwether, Wallace, and the Internet Archive policy page for the tape-trading and sound-archive ecosystem [marshall_bootleg_collectors; jstor_daily_tape_trading; dead_net_taping_dead; wallace_cocreation_sound_archive; internet_archive_gd_collection_policy], Brackett’s Live Dead for liveness and recorded-performance scholarship [brackett_live_dead], Adams and Sardiello for Deadhead sociology [adams_deadhead_social_science], Dodd and Weiner for the bibliographic field map [dodd_weiner_annotated_bibliography], Britannica and Americana Highways for narrow biographical and studio-reception context [britannica_jerry_garcia; americana_highways_workingmans_american_beauty], and official public-recognition sources for later institutional status [rockhall_grateful_dead; grammy_lifetime_achievement; kennedy_center_honors_2024]. Community reconstructions such as touring revenue estimates are cited only as labelled business-history context, not as compendium measurements [grateful_seconds_touring_revenues].

What is in this repository

We ship a committed archival compendium under data/archival/ that drives every analysis and category-theoretic claim in this paper end-to-end, plus a parser for each cited source so that refreshing the snapshot is a matter of running the same code with online flags.

Related work

Prior Grateful Dead data efforts are mostly single-source or single-purpose. Community setlist databases and the gdshowsdb / truckin-through-time projects [gdshowsdb; noah_truckin_through_time] assemble the raw show record; aggregators such as Grateful Stats [grateful_stats] and community spreadsheets and forums [reddit_setlist_db; reddit_data_sets] surface frequency rankings; and query libraries such as jerryPycia [jerry_pycia] and the grateful-data tutorial [grateful_data_tutorial] expose one source programmatically. Archive and popular-music studies add a second frame: the Dead’s sound record is co-produced by band, archive, tapers, uploaders, and curators rather than handed down by a single authority [wallace_cocreation_sound_archive; dead_net_taping_dead; internet_archive_gd_collection_policy; brackett_live_dead]. Sociological work on Deadheads, bibliographic scholarship, and tape-trader studies explain why this record is also a community institution rather than simply a discography [adams_deadhead_social_science; dodd_weiner_annotated_bibliography; marshall_bootleg_collectors; jstor_daily_tape_trading].

This compendium differs on two axes. It is integration-first: primary sources are reconciled on canonical slugs into one immutable, citation-bound schema with a runtime honesty boundary and completeness/figure-validation probes. It is also structure-first: the performance record is read through small but mechanically checked categorical constructions (functors, spans, colimits, a date-poset Yoneda statement) rather than treated only as a frequency table. The music-theory lineage is Lewin’s transformational shift from static objects to relations and transformations [lewin_gmit], with newer categorical music work showing how such networks can be formalized diagrammatically [padi_category_music_case_study; popoff_hidden_categories]. Our contribution is a concrete, reproducible instantiation on a real performance corpus, with every quantitative or categorical claim bound to code.

What is not in this repository

We do not bundle lyric text — full lyrics are copyrighted. The compendium stores songs as titled records with composer and lyricist attribution; the semantic layer (motifs, themes, annotations) is read against external indices such as Alex Allan’s finder and Dodd’s annotated lyrics [whitegum_alex_allan; dodd_annotated_lyrics]. We do not bundle audio. The audio-enriched category in §3 is a structural abstraction over similarity weights; concrete weights are produced by an external pipeline against the fifteen-songs-dataset [fifteen_songs_dataset].
