Abstract

We present a modular, citation-bound data compendium for the Grateful Dead universe — shows, songs, performances, personnel timelines, venues, recordings, and reception — and a category-theoretic interpretation of the performance graph. The work is grounded in the archival reality that Grateful Dead history is both institutional and participatory: UCSC’s Grateful Dead Archive and the Internet Archive collection preserve formal and community records [ucsc_grateful_dead_archive; internet_archive_lma; internet_archive_gd_collection_policy], while taping and trading scholarship shows why setlists and recording metadata are cultural evidence, not merely fan trivia [dead_net_taping_dead; wallace_cocreation_sound_archive]. The surrounding source dossier also binds the non-quantitative historical frame – formation and Acid Test context, Wall of Sound engineering, live recording/liveness scholarship, Deadhead sociology, studio-era reception, and public recognition – to checked sources rather than to folklore alone [dead_net_biography; foundsf_weir_psychedelic_sf; smithsonian_institutionally_dead; mcintosh_wall_of_sound; brackett_live_dead; adams_deadhead_social_science; rockhall_grateful_dead; grammy_lifetime_achievement; kennedy_center_honors_2024]. The compendium integrates nine primary sources (Setlist.fm [setlistfm_gd], The SetList Program [setlists_net_program], the Mark Leone CMU setlist archive [cmu_leone_setlists; noah_truckin_through_time], GDsets [gdsets_com; gdsets_dead], gdshowsdb [gdshowsdb; github_topic_gratefuldead], the Internet Archive Live Music Archive [internet_archive_lma; fifteen_songs_dataset], the Alex Allan / whitegum lyric finder [whitegum_alex_allan; cmu_leone_lyrics], the official band site [dead_net_official], and Wikipedia [wikipedia_gd; wikipedia_discography]) with four reference sources (Britannica [britannica_gd], the lineup-changes guide [ucr_lineup_changes], Dodd and Trist’s The Complete Annotated Grateful Dead Lyrics [dodd_annotated_lyrics], and the Grateful Stats front-end [grateful_stats]) and secondary corpora and community discussions [grateful_data_tutorial; jerry_pycia; gd_reviews_corpus; reddit_data_sets; reddit_lyrics_attribution; reddit_setlist_db; reddit_spreadsheet_archive]. Each source is parsed by an independently testable reference module written against the documented record shape; the committed compendium under data/archival/ is the dataset reported here (3341 ingested shows (gdshowsdb + truckin gap-fill; community literature estimates ~2318 canonical concerts), 645 songs, 912 venues, 40757 performance rows). A runtime completeness audit and figure-validation gate certify referential integrity and non-degenerate outputs on every pipeline run. Integration is a deterministic, sort-keyed merge over canonical slugs; registered figures also emit CSV/JSON data tables, and a first-principles claim ledger classifies each major result by irreducible input, hard constraint, assumption, validation artifact, and interpretation limit. Exploratory repertoire/uncertainty panels are labelled as pattern-discovery rather than causal inference. We then exhibit four small but real categorical constructions, situated against transformational and categorical music-theory precedents [lewin_gmit; padi_category_music_case_study; popoff_hidden_categories]: a poset category of dates, a discrete category of shows, a monotone cumulative setlist functor and lineup functor from dates into sets, and a span representation that takes each performance to be the apex of a span between its show and its song. Wide pullbacks over a fixed show recover the show’s setlist; wide pullbacks over a fixed song recover the song’s performance history. The active-band roster, by contrast, is a non-monotone presheaf on the date poset — a categorical formalization of the familiar fact that members come and go. The artefacts in this paper come from the committed archival snapshot; all source-ingestion modules are written against the real source shape so that scripts/00_fetch_sources.py --online --write-archival refreshes the full snapshot.
