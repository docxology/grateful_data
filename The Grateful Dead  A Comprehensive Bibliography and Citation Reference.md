# The Grateful Dead: A Comprehensive Bibliography and Citation Reference

## Overview

The Grateful Dead—formed in 1965 in the San Francisco Bay Area and active through 1995—are one of the most extensively documented, studied, and cited musical acts in the history of American popular culture. This report compiles all major scholarly, journalistic, archival, and interdisciplinary citations and sources related to the band, organized by category, format, and chronology. Drawing on the official bibliography of the Grateful Dead Studies Association, the Duke University Press *Studies in the Grateful Dead* series, library research guides, and independent scholarship across sociology, musicology, law, religion, business, and cultural studies, this document constitutes a comprehensive reference for researchers, students, and archivists.[^1][^2]

## Part I: Band History and Formation

### Origins and Formation (1965)

The Grateful Dead trace their origins to a Palo Alto, California jug band called **Mother McCree's Uptown Jug Champions**, which included Jerry Garcia (lead guitar, vocals), Bob Weir (rhythm guitar, vocals), and Ron "Pigpen" McKernan (keyboards, harmonica, vocals). After the band converted to electric instruments in 1965—inspired by watching the Beatles film *A Hard Day's Night*—they added Phil Lesh (bass, vocals) and Bill Kreutzmann (drums), and became known as The Warlocks.[^3][^4][^1]

The band formally renamed itself the Grateful Dead in late November or early December 1965. According to Phil Lesh's autobiography, Jerry Garcia randomly opened an old *Britannica World Language Dictionary* and read the phrase aloud; Garcia described the name as "a stunning combination." Alan Trist, director of the band's music publishing company Ice Nine, placed the discovery in the Funk & Wagnalls *Folklore Dictionary* during a game of "Fictionary." The term *grateful dead* derives from a widespread cross-cultural folk motif describing a traveler who pays the burial debts of a neglected corpse and is later protected or aided by that corpse's spirit. A parallel phrasing also appears in the *Egyptian Book of the Dead*.[^5][^6][^7][^8][^9]

The band performed its first show as the Grateful Dead on **December 4, 1965**, at a Ken Kesey-organized "Acid Test" in San Jose, California—the first public event of that name. Kesey had scheduled the date to coincide with a Rolling Stones concert seven blocks away. LSD manufacturer and sound pioneer **Owsley Stanley** (Augustus Owsley Stanley III) bankrolled the early band. The band lived communally at **710 Ashbury Street** in San Francisco's Haight-Ashbury district from 1966 to 1968, where they were arrested during a 1967 marijuana raid and subsequently held a press conference calling for decriminalization.[^10][^4][^11][^12][^3][^5]

### Disbandment and Legacy

Ron "Pigpen" McKernan died on March 8, 1973, from liver failure due to alcoholism at age 27. Jerry Garcia died on August 9, 1995, at a rehabilitation facility in Forest Knolls, California, of a heart attack at age 53. The band's final concert with Garcia took place on **July 9, 1995**, at Chicago's Soldier Field. The band officially dissolved four months after Garcia's death, in late 1995. Despite its dissolution, the Grateful Dead legacy has persisted and, by some estimates, the active Deadhead community is larger today than it was during the band's lifetime.[^15][^16][^17][^3]

In 2025, Dead & Company (featuring Bob Weir, Mickey Hart, John Mayer, Oteil Burbridge, Jeff Chimenti, and Jay Lane) marked the **60th anniversary** of the Grateful Dead with a three-night concert series at Golden Gate Park, San Francisco (August 1–3, 2025). The band was also named **MusiCares Person of the Year** and received a **Kennedy Center Honor** in late 2024.[^18][^19][^20][^21][^22]

## Part III: The Grateful Dead Archive

The **Grateful Dead Archive** was donated to the University of California, Santa Cruz by the surviving band members in April 2008. Bob Weir stated: "It seemed to all of us that the stuff really belongs to the community that supported us for all those years." The archive is housed in Special Collections & Archives at McHenry Library, UCSC, and comprises nearly **a thousand boxes** of documents, recordings, photographs, business records, show files, fan mail, backstage passes, flyers, posters, stage backdrops, stained-glass pieces, and props. The majority of documents date from 1970–1995.[^29][^30][^31][^32][^33]

**Grateful Dead Archive Online** — UC Santa Cruz, in collaboration with the California Digital Library and the Internet Archive, has digitized and made publicly available over **45,000 items** from the collection.[^32][^33]

- Mailing Address: Special Collections and Archives, McHenry Library, University of California, Santa Cruz, 1156 High St., Santa Cruz CA 95064

The Grateful Dead's **taping policy**—formally codified in the 1980s and extended to digital distribution in the internet era—constitutes a landmark case study in alternative intellectual property strategy. The policy permitted fans to record concerts and trade recordings noncommercially, with no commercial gain permitted to hosting websites. Lyricist John Perry Barlow directly attributed the band's commercial success to this policy of free cultural circulation.[^69][^70][^67]
